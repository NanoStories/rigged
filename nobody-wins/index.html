Nobody Wins

Fill every cell red or blue so that red can’t cross from top to bottom and blue can’t cross from left to right.

Where this comes from

The House is relying on the Hex theorem: on a completely filled board, exactly one color connects its two sides. The board comes from Hex, a game invented by the Danish mathematician and poet Piet Hein, who introduced it in 1942. The newspaper Politiken published it that December under the name Polygon. John Nash came up with the same game again at Princeton in 1948 or 1949.

Because a game of Hex can never end in a draw, the player who moves first always has a winning strategy. Nash proved this with a famously sneaky argument that never says what the strategy actually is.

In 1979 David Gale showed that the Hex theorem is equivalent to Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem, one of the landmarks of topology. It’s often pictured with a cup of coffee: stir it however you like without splashing, and once it settles, at least one point of the coffee is exactly where it started.
