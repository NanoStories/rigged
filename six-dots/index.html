Six Dots

Color every line red or blue without making a triangle that’s all one color.

Give every dot exactly two red lines and two blue lines.

Where this comes from

You just lost to the smallest case of Ramsey’s theorem, published by the British mathematician Frank Ramsey in 1930. Its message: color a big enough network and some part of it is forced into perfect order. Six dots are enough to force a one-color triangle. Five aren’t, as the warm-up showed.

The House was underselling it, too. Every coloring of six dots has at least two one-color triangles, never just one.

The numbers grow alarmingly fast. To force four dots all joined in one color, you need 18 dots. To force five, you need somewhere between 43 and 46, and nobody knows the exact number. Paul Erdős liked to say that if aliens threatened to destroy Earth unless we told them that number, we should put every computer and mathematician to work on it. If they asked for the version with six, we should try to destroy the aliens instead.
